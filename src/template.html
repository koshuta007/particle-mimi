What this is

Your knowledge-quest phrasal verb trainer, rebuilt as one file you own. Same 431 verbs, same Leitner boxes, same drill format — but the practice happens here instead of in Telegram, and the data is repaired.

The boxes

Every verb sits in a box, and the box decides how long until you see it again: Box 0 new or missed (comes straight back), 1 after a day, 2 after three, 3 after a week, 4 after two weeks, 5 after a month. Answer right and the verb moves up one box. Answer wrong and it moves down one — not all the way to the floor. A verb you have carried to Box 4 and then fumble goes back to Box 3, so you see it in a week rather than starting it over.

When is a verb finished?

When it reaches Box 5. That is the whole rule. A verb that climbs all the way to Box 5 counts as mastered, leaves the deck, and is never asked again. Nothing to re-prove, no maintenance cycle.

Getting there takes five correct answers in a row with the gaps widening each time — a day, three days, a week, a fortnight. Miss it anywhere along the way and it drops a box and has to climb again.

One thing worth knowing, because it is measurable in your own history: over six months, 23% of the verbs that first reached Box 5 were missed the very next time they came up. Under the old cycling rule those got caught and sent back down. Under this rule they do not — once a verb is in, it is in. If you later want a safety net, turn off Box 5 means finished in Settings and set a re-check cycle; the verbs already finished come straight back into circulation.

The round

Rounds are run with Claude in chat, not on this page. This page is where you see where you stand; after each round the data is updated and the page is republished at the same link.

  • Thirty verbs. Due reviews first, then Box 0 to fill up.
  • Never only Box 0 — that would waste the spacing.
  • Miss one and you get an immediate side-drill on it, while the memory is fresh. The side-drill does not change the box.
  • Verbs you keep missing become dragons and get their own round.
  • Every round ends with the full mistakes list and a fresh sentence for each one.

The settings on this page record that format, and they travel with the exported data.

Why the prompt shows a sentence

Thirty-six of the prompts in this deck have more than one right answer — return alone fits bring back, come back, get back, give back, go back, hand back and head back. Asking on the bare word marks you wrong for knowing a second answer. So the prompt is the meaning and a sentence with a hole in it — the sentence decides which one fits. If you type a different valid verb, it says so instead of failing you.

Moving the data
